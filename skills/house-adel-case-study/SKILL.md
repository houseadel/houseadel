# House Adel Case Study

## Establish the evidence

1. Read `AGENTS.md`, the project brief, decision record, asset manifest, test results, and approved credits.
2. Confirm whether the work is commissioned, self-initiated, speculative, or an experiment. State this near the beginning.
3. Confirm publication rights, client confidentiality, collaborator approval, and asset attribution before exposing process material.
4. Separate verified facts, House Adel's interpretation, and retrospective lessons. Mark unavailable information instead of inventing it.

## Build the narrative

Structure the case study around the project's actual logic rather than a decorative template:

1. **Concept:** state the governing idea in plain language.
2. **Context:** explain the audience, occasion or business need, constraints, and scope.
3. **Role:** identify exactly what House Adel and every collaborator did.
4. **Creative direction:** connect typography, imagery, pacing, sound, and world-building choices to the idea.
5. **Asset production:** show the master-frame process, sources, tools, transformations, rights, and human decisions.
6. **Interaction:** explain discovery, navigation, motion, transition, and fallback behavior.
7. **Development:** document only verified architecture, rendering, motion, content, testing, and deployment choices.
8. **Mobile:** show how the experience was reinterpreted rather than merely reduced.
9. **Accessibility:** show semantic equivalents, keyboard and focus behavior, reduced motion, audio control, and failure fallbacks.
10. **Performance:** report measured budgets, devices, network conditions, frame behavior, and material optimizations.
11. **Outcomes:** report sourced results with dates and measurement context; use qualitative delivery facts when metrics do not exist.
12. **Credits:** name people, roles, tools, licensed material, and relevant partners accurately.
13. **Behind the scenes:** include sketches, rejected routes, prototypes, production constraints, and lessons only when they clarify authorship.

Omit or combine empty sections, but never omit a material limitation to make the project appear stronger.

## Write for discovery and depth

- Give visitors the concept, context, role, and project status within the opening section.
- Lead with the project's idea, not a list of technologies.
- Use short explanatory passages beside the evidence they interpret.
- Distinguish House Adel's editorial system from the client's aesthetic.
- Explain how choices served the brief; do not narrate every production step.
- Make the work remain useful after the first 30 seconds through real context, decisions, process, credits, and outcomes.
- Label technical uncertainty as `Inference:` and never claim a framework, tool, metric, or responsibility without evidence.

## Pair media with resilient content

Give each image, video, animation, canvas, and audio item an editorial purpose. Provide accurate alt text or mark decorative media appropriately; add captions, transcripts, posters, mobile variants, reduced-motion alternatives, and static fallbacks where required. Keep essential explanations in semantic HTML.

Preserve a direct project URL, meaningful page title and heading, semantic previous/next or archive links, and a clear route back to the nexus or work index. Verify keyboard access and browser Back/Forward behavior.

## Review before publication

Check every name, role, date, credit, URL, technology claim, metric, license, and quotation against its source. Confirm that performance and accessibility statements cite test evidence and that outcome numbers include their timeframe and basis.

Reject vague agency language, inflated roles, invented impact, undisclosed experiments, technology theatre, decorative process filler, or client work presented as House Adel's own aesthetic. Record remaining confidentiality, evidence, accessibility, performance, and asset-rights gaps before approval.

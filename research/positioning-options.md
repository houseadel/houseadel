# House Adel positioning options

**Status:** Options for creative-direction review; **none is selected**  
**Research date:** 2026-07-30  
**Evidence base:** [`market-positioning.md`](market-positioning.md), [`competitor-map.md`](competitor-map.md), and [`service-models.md`](service-models.md)  
**Source access date:** All linked web sources were accessed 2026-07-30.

## Decision rule

The final position has to answer four questions in language a buyer can repeat:

1. What kind of studio is House Adel?
2. Which consequential moments is it for?
3. What does it deliver that a template, platform, or conventional web supplier does not?
4. Why is a small Indonesian studio credible for this work?

The multiverse is evaluated *inside* each option. It is not allowed to decide the business position by itself.

## Shared evidence constraining every option

- Functional wedding websites can be free, while design-forward platforms cost tens of dollars per month. [Joy pricing](https://withjoy.com/pricing/), [Bliss & Bone pricing](https://blissandbone.com/wedding-website/features-and-pricing), [Riley & Grey pricing](https://www.rileygrey.com/pricing)
- Indonesia’s invitation market includes feature-rich offers below Rp500,000 and itemized custom-design/illustration upgrades. [Wedly](https://wedly.id/), [Connectied proposal](https://connectied.id/proposal.pdf)
- Music pre-save, release routing, ticket links, fan data, and analytics are productized. [Spotify Countdown Pages](https://artists.spotify.com/countdown-pages), [Linkfire pricing](https://www.linkfire.com/pricing), [Music Glue](https://www.musicglue.com/)
- Gallery inventory, websites, marketing, sales and payments are productized through Artlogic. [Artlogic](https://artlogic.net/)
- Restaurant menus, reservations, ordering, payments, and responsive templates are standard platform features. [Squarespace Restaurants](https://www.squarespace.com/tour/restaurant-websites), [Wix Restaurants](https://www.wix.com/restaurant/website), [Tock](https://www.squarespace.com/tock)
- A realistic small creative-web peer, Rhumb Studio, publishes fixed-fee starting points near €2,000 for a landing-page sprint and €12,000 for full strategy/design/build work, while selling strategy, UX/UI, development and agency support internationally. [Rhumb Studio](https://www.rhumb.co/)
- Premium reference work joins a governing idea to assets, interaction, operations, and multiple touchpoints: Studio Dumbar’s DEMO, Linked by Air’s Cooper Hewitt platform, and Stink Studios’ Chanel campaign are direct examples. [DEMO 2019](https://studiodumbar.com/work/demo), [Cooper Hewitt](https://www.linkedbyair.net/work/132-cooper-hewitt-smithsonian-design-museum), [Chanel Chance](https://www.stinkstudios.com/work/chance)

The common implication is that House Adel cannot sell generic functionality or “immersive” effects. Every viable option needs authored concept, asset direction, appropriate integration, and trustworthy launch delivery.

## Option 1 — The Private Occasion Atelier

### Exact description

**House Adel is a digital atelier for singular private occasions, creating bespoke invitation worlds and guest experiences that unite story, imagery, typography, motion, and event identity.**

This is intentionally narrower than “wedding website studio.” The object of sale is the occasion world; the website is its mobile operational center.

### Target clients

- Affluent destination-wedding couples represented by a planner;
- private hosts and family offices working through event producers;
- luxury resorts, villas, and venues;
- wedding/event planners wanting a premium white-label digital partner;
- stationers and event designers needing a digital counterpart.

The Bali Wedding Association directory confirms a local professional network spanning planners, international-facing venues, photographers, decorators, caterers and other wedding suppliers. [Bali Wedding Association member directory](https://baliweddingassociation.com/member-directory/)

### Flagship deliverables

- Bespoke digital invitation/guest experience;
- occasion visual bible and master frame;
- save-the-date, itinerary, invitation and share assets;
- RSVP/check-in integration;
- coordinated print-ready and venue-screen pieces;
- bilingual/international guest treatment;
- post-event digital keepsake or archive.

### Competitive advantage to prove

- Direct creative direction rather than template selection;
- a concept derived from the people, place and ritual;
- one coherent event suite across digital and physical touchpoints;
- regional knowledge plus international-facing service;
- limited commissions and high-touch curation.

### Advantages

- Closest to Marshall’s demonstrated interests in invitations, luxury/editorial art direction, typography, imagery and atmosphere.
- A bounded occasion provides a clear brief, audience and immovable launch date.
- Bali and Indonesia offer an existing planner/venue partnership ecosystem.
- Each commission can visibly demonstrate a radically different world.
- Planner and venue channels can generate repeat work even when couples do not.

### Weakness

- The category is price-anchored by free global products and very inexpensive Indonesian vendors.
- Direct clients are mostly one-off.
- “Atelier” can sound elegant but obscure, overly precious, or like stationery/fashion rather than digital work.
- Private work may have limited case-study rights.
- High-touch service and original assets can consume more time than clients expect from the word “invitation.”

### Market risks

- Being perceived as a costly version of Bliss & Bone, Riley & Grey, Wedly or Connectied.
- Generic luxury styling—black, cream, gold, serif type, ambient music—substituting for a real idea.
- Operational failures in RSVP, privacy or guest information damaging trust.
- A portfolio dominated by romance making cultural and commercial expansion harder.

### Portfolio implications

The portfolio would need:

- 3–5 occasion cases or specimens with distinctly different concepts;
- full suites, not cropped homepage heroes;
- mobile guest flows and logistical clarity;
- world bibles, asset provenance, paper/digital relationship and partner credits;
- at least one bilingual or destination case;
- a discreet private-work presentation option;
- one adjacent public event or hospitality case to demonstrate expansion potential.

House Adel’s own identity should feel like a house of commissioned occasions, not another wedding aesthetic.

### Technical implications

- Mostly semantic DOM with restrained motion; WebGL only when the concept genuinely requires it.
- Robust private/indexing controls, RSVP integration, share previews, maps, calendar files and international typography.
- Mobile and slow-network behavior are primary, not fallbacks.
- A reusable operational shell is valuable, but every client-facing visual system must be newly authored.
- Data should live in a specialist system where possible; House Adel should not own unnecessary guest PII.

### Likely revenue model

- Fixed-fee commission after a paid direction sprint;
- partner/venue package or white-label fee;
- specialist and asset costs passed through transparently;
- post-event archive and limited care add-ons.

**Planning hypothesis:** USD 3,000–8,000 for the authored digital core, subject to proposal testing; not a market benchmark or approved price.

### Repeat-client potential

- Low from couples;
- high from planners, venues, stationers and event producers;
- medium from private hosts with recurring family/institutional occasions.

### Partnership opportunities

Wedding planners, venue groups, event designers, stationers, photographers/filmmakers, illustrators, calligraphers/type designers, translators, RSVP/check-in providers and print producers.

### Path to commercial microsites

Occasion world → planner/venue signature offer → public event identity → hospitality opening → agency-supported luxury launch.

The bridge is strongest when case studies show concept across touchpoints, not only wedding imagery.

### Multiverse fit

**Fit: Moderate, with a major tonal caveat.**

The multiverse can express one atelier entering different personal realities. It supports radical visual change without making one wedding style the House Adel identity. However:

- “multiverse” can feel grand, fictional or game-like against intimate human occasions;
- shattered glass implies rupture, danger or brokenness and is especially questionable for weddings;
- private clients need immediate trust and clarity more than a cryptic nexus.

If this option is selected, a softer model—rooms, letters, thresholds, constellations, cabinets, chapters, or an invitation house—may express plurality better. The current shattered-fragment nexus is **not supported by the market fit alone**.

### Evidence needed before selection

- Three planner interviews and two venue interviews;
- willingness-to-pay tests above local custom-design add-ons;
- case-study/privacy expectations;
- one complete specimen reviewed by working planners;
- evidence that “digital atelier” is understood without explanation.

## Option 2 — The Cultural Editions Studio

### Exact description

**House Adel is an independent web studio for artists, exhibitions, releases, and cultural editions—turning each body of work into a distinct, accessible digital form.**

“Edition” is the organizing idea: a bounded cultural release with a reason to exist now and a considered life afterward.

### Target clients

- Independent labels and artist management;
- established regional musicians and visual artists;
- galleries, curators, foundations and artist estates;
- publishers, fashion/culture collaborations and design festivals;
- universities and small cultural institutions;
- brands sponsoring a serious cultural program.

### Flagship deliverables

- Album/release worlds;
- exhibition companions and digital exhibitions;
- artist archives or anniversary editions;
- editorial microsites and digital publications;
- tour, program or festival chapters;
- integration with streaming, ticketing, stores, Artlogic or a client CMS.

### Competitive advantage to prove

- Editorial and curatorial sensitivity combined with implementation;
- a willingness to let the material determine structure and interaction;
- atmosphere without hiding credits, essays, works or listening paths;
- one senior director across concept, assets and web;
- a reusable edition framework that does not homogenize content.

Linked by Air’s Cooper Hewitt platform is relevant evidence: its interaction model reflects curatorial exploration while remaining reusable for future exhibitions. [Cooper Hewitt digital exhibitions](https://www.linkedbyair.net/work/132-cooper-hewitt-smithsonian-design-museum)

### Advantages

- Strong fit with editorial art direction, storytelling, typography, curation and radically changing project worlds.
- Releases and exhibitions have natural chapters, archives and repeat cycles.
- Cultural work can establish authorship and attract aligned commercial collaborators.
- Functional infrastructure can be integrated rather than rebuilt.
- “Edition” gives compactness a positive form instead of implying a small agency.

### Weakness

- Many independent artists and small galleries have limited cash budgets.
- Cultural stakeholders may require extensive content, rights, accessibility and institutional approvals.
- Outcomes may be cultural rather than easily quantified.
- The line between design, curatorial interpretation and authorship must be carefully credited.
- The studio could become known for low-paid passion projects.

### Market risks

- Spotify and Linkfire make a basic release page unnecessary; Artlogic and standard CMS products make a basic gallery site unnecessary.
- “Immersive artist website” can become a vanity object that impedes listening, reading or viewing.
- Grants and institutional schedules can be slow and administratively heavy.
- Rights for art, film, music, archives and type can delay or prevent publication.

### Portfolio implications

The portfolio would need:

- at least one album/release experience and one exhibition/editorial experience;
- rich credits and role descriptions;
- evidence of content depth after the hero;
- listening/viewing/reading paths that remain immediate;
- an archive demonstrating what happens after launch;
- process showing how form came from the work;
- at least one repeat edition or reusable system;
- honest outcomes such as attendance, streams/click-through, press, dwell, archive use or curator feedback where available.

### Technical implications

- Strong structured-content and CMS discipline;
- deep links to works, tracks, essays and events;
- media rights metadata, captions, transcripts and alt text;
- Linkfire/Spotify/Music Glue, ticketing, store and Artlogic integrations;
- accessible audio/video controls;
- durable URLs and archive/sunset behavior;
- optional WebGL or spatial navigation only when it expresses the material and preserves a complete semantic route.

### Likely revenue model

- Fixed fee per edition;
- paid direction/editorial-architecture sprint;
- label roster or gallery season retainer;
- annual archive/edition maintenance;
- sponsor or grant-funded cultural commissions.

**Planning hypothesis:** USD 4,500–14,000 per bounded edition, with curatorial content and original asset production separate.

### Repeat-client potential

- Medium for an individual artist;
- high for management, labels, galleries, festivals and institutions with programs;
- high for archives and anniversaries once trust is established.

### Partnership opportunities

Curators, editors, label designers, artist managers, photographers/directors, sound and motion designers, type designers, rights administrators, Linkfire/Music Glue/Artlogic specialists and cultural producers.

### Path to commercial microsites

Cultural edition → sponsored artist collaboration → fashion/beverage/culture partnership → entertainment campaign → commercial release world.

The route is credible when House Adel can show audience action and production reliability, not only cultural taste.

### Multiverse fit

**Fit: Strong in principle.**

Different bodies of work naturally constitute different realities, while House Adel provides the relationship among them. Editions also give the multiverse a time dimension: new worlds arrive without retroactively restyling old ones.

Risks:

- the nexus can compete with the artists’ work;
- shattered fragments can impose the same metaphor on unrelated cultural material;
- a game-like portal selector can trivialize scholarship or accessibility;
- radical route differences need a stable archive, credits and contact layer.

A library, constellation, index of editions, changing exhibition architecture, or material archive may be more defensible than shattered glass. The current nexus remains a prototype hypothesis.

### Evidence needed before selection

- Interviews with two label/management teams and three curators/galleries;
- proof of real budgets or funding sources;
- rights and CMS workflow test;
- one release and one exhibition specimen;
- validation that “edition” is understood by both music and visual-culture buyers.

## Option 3 — The Launch-World Studio

### Exact description

**House Adel creates compact, art-directed digital worlds for hospitality, fragrance, beauty, fashion, object, and brand launches—focused experiences that make one new thing felt and easy to act on.**

This is the most explicitly commercial option. It does not offer permanent corporate sites, ecommerce platforms, or full-service marketing.

### Target clients

- Independent fragrance, beauty, jewelry, fashion, food/drink and design-object brands;
- chef-led restaurants, boutique hotels, villas and hospitality groups;
- regional distributors;
- funded founders launching one hero product/place;
- PR, brand and campaign agencies seeking a digital craft partner.

### Flagship deliverables

- Product/place launch microsite;
- teaser/countdown and live-launch states;
- sensory product story or place narrative;
- film, photography, motion and sound integration;
- retailer, stockist, ecommerce, booking, map or reservation pathways;
- social/press motion assets;
- localized campaign chapters;
- post-launch archive or redirect.

### Competitive advantage to prove

- Luxury/editorial art direction without a fixed “luxury look”;
- concept, assets, interaction and code handled as one compact system;
- senior attention and a small partner-shaped team;
- higher distinctiveness than a landing-page supplier, lower coordination burden than assembling several freelancers;
- disciplined integration rather than unnecessary platform replacement.

### Advantages

- Buyers have a commercial launch reason, deadline, distribution and potential measurement.
- Strong fit with Marshall’s interests in luxury/editorial art direction, photography, atmosphere and campaign thinking.
- Repeat product, menu, property and seasonal launches are possible.
- A compact experience can sit beside an existing ecommerce or booking platform.
- Agency partnerships offer a credible international entry.

### Weakness

- Excellent photography, film, CGI, motion, copy and sometimes sound are expensive and essential.
- Direct luxury clients expect proven production, brand governance, security, localization and service.
- House Adel currently lacks public evidence of delivering high-traffic or multimarket launches.
- One person cannot credibly provide every specialist craft implied by this option.
- “Digital world” can sound like WebGL spectacle instead of a commercial tool.

### Market risks

- Competing with integrated studios such as Stink Studios, Locomotive or OFF+BRAND at a resource level House Adel does not possess.
- Generic AI product imagery or inaccurate pack shots creating brand and rights risk.
- The permanent ecommerce/booking experience making a standalone microsite unnecessary.
- Asset and approval delays colliding with fixed launch dates.
- Scope expanding into full brand identity, content production, social campaign, ecommerce and media without matching fee/team.

Stink’s Chanel case demonstrates the full-scale buying unit—strategy, concept, interactive film, mobile/tablet, 24 languages, display and a region-specific activation—but is explicitly a future-scale reference. [Stink Studios, Chanel Chance](https://www.stinkstudios.com/work/chance)

### Portfolio implications

The portfolio would need:

- 3–4 launch cases/specimens across at least two sectors;
- product/place accuracy and strong source assets;
- teaser, launch and post-launch states;
- clear commercial action and analytics plan;
- mobile performance and static fallback;
- asset rights/provenance and specialist credits;
- a case showing integration with an existing booking/store system;
- restrained case-study claims unless outcome evidence exists.

### Technical implications

- Campaign-state management and scheduled publication;
- high-fidelity responsive media with strict loading budgets;
- ecommerce/retailer/booking integration rather than replacement;
- consent, analytics, campaign tags and share metadata;
- localization-ready content and typography;
- traffic/load planning;
- optional 2.5D/WebGL with capped quality tiers and static/video alternatives;
- reliable archive/redirect after the paid-media window.

### Likely revenue model

- Paid direction/asset-feasibility sprint;
- fixed launch fee;
- specialist/asset production pass-through plus management;
- burst retainer from teaser through post-launch;
- agency reserved-capacity agreement;
- later, seasonal or portfolio launch retainer.

**Planning hypothesis:** USD 8,000–24,000 for a compact hospitality/product launch and USD 10,000–30,000 for a more campaign-intensive microsite. These are proposal experiments, not published prices.

### Repeat-client potential

- Medium with a single founder/place;
- high with product portfolios, hospitality groups, distributors, PR firms and agencies;
- very high if House Adel becomes a dependable specialist partner.

### Partnership opportunities

Brand and packaging studios, PR/campaign agencies, photographers/directors, CGI/product-visualization artists, stylists, sound/motion designers, hospitality consultants, booking/ecommerce implementers and regional translators.

### Path from invitations

Destination invitation → venue relationship → restaurant/villa opening → product collaboration → agency-supported launch.

This is a credible path because occasions and launches share anticipation, art direction, fixed dates, guest/customer action and cross-touchpoint assets. It fails if the portfolio only demonstrates wedding motifs.

### Multiverse fit

**Fit: Strongest literal fit, highest cliché risk.**

Each product/place can be a distinct world, and the nexus can dramatize House Adel’s ability to enter unlike realities. This aligns closely with the current premise.

However:

- “brand worlds” is common agency language and can become empty;
- portals, shards, glow and cinematic fly-throughs can resemble gaming or entertainment menus;
- the portfolio may overpromise custom 3D and production depth;
- clients need a fast path to capabilities, evidence and contact.

If selected, the multiverse must be explained as **an operating range demonstrated through real launch logic**, not as a spectacle. Shattered glass should survive concept, mobile, accessibility and client-comprehension testing independently.

### Evidence needed before selection

- Interviews with two hospitality founders, two PR/brand agencies and two product founders;
- asset-budget and quote testing;
- one hospitality and one product specimen with real integrations;
- specialist partner roster and sample contracts;
- evidence that buyers understand “launch world” as a focused commercial experience rather than a metaverse.

## Option 4 — The Creative-Development Partner

### Exact description

**House Adel is a senior creative-development partner for design, brand, event, and campaign teams—translating ambitious art direction into focused, responsive, production-ready web experiences.**

The lead buyer is another professional team, not the end client. Creative direction remains part of the offer, but collaboration and delivery clarity lead the positioning.

### Target clients

- Brand and identity studios;
- advertising and PR agencies;
- event and experience producers;
- photographers/directors and motion studios;
- independent designers needing a trusted build partner;
- in-house creative teams with campaign overflow.

### Flagship deliverables

- Creative-development direction and feasibility;
- interaction and motion prototypes;
- responsive implementation of approved art direction;
- compact campaign microsites;
- portfolio, exhibition, release and event builds;
- WebGL/2.5D integration where justified;
- accessibility, performance, browser QA, launch and handover.

### Competitive advantage to prove

- Marshall can discuss the idea and protect art direction, not merely translate a Figma file;
- direct senior collaboration;
- compact scope and fewer handoffs;
- honest specialist escalation;
- strong mobile, accessibility, performance and interruption behavior;
- capacity to give unlike projects unlike digital behavior.

### Advantages

- Most realistic route to international commercial work without claiming direct access to global brands.
- Partners bring clients, strategy, assets, media and some project management.
- Repeat relationships can smooth revenue.
- Work can span all requested sectors without vague direct-client messaging.
- Build-only and R&D scopes can be bounded.

### Weakness

- “Creative development” foregrounds a capability Marshall does not yet claim as senior-specialist expertise without Codex and partners.
- It can underplay Marshall’s creative direction and reduce House Adel to implementation.
- White-label terms can hide the strongest work.
- Partner margins and procurement can compress fees.
- The studio becomes dependent on other teams’ design quality, content discipline and sales pipeline.

### Market risks

- Overpromising advanced WebGL, shaders, 3D or animation.
- Supplied designs failing responsive/accessibility requirements and House Adel absorbing the correction.
- Unclear responsibility between agency, end client and studio.
- Being hired late after impossible promises have already been made.
- Portfolio permissions preventing direct-market credibility.

OFF+BRAND’s 39-person team page is a useful capacity warning: its brand-to-platform promise is supported by designers, strategists, project managers, CMS specialists, motion, 3D and WebGL roles. [OFF+BRAND about](https://www.itsoffbrand.com/about-us) House Adel’s promise must remain smaller and explicit.

### Portfolio implications

The portfolio would need:

- role and credit clarity on every case;
- supplied-design versus co-created concept distinctions;
- technical breakdowns understandable to professional partners;
- prototypes and failure/reduced-motion behavior;
- evidence of respecting different design systems;
- launch/QA documentation;
- partner testimonials emphasizing reliability and judgment;
- a private credentials deck for NDA work.

The public site can still be art-directed, but procurement information—capabilities, role, process, availability and contact—must be immediate.

### Technical implications

- Strong front-end architecture, source control, handoff and documentation;
- compatibility with partner design and CMS stacks;
- browser/device and accessibility test automation;
- clean boundaries for direct Three.js or React Three Fiber after the architecture decision;
- staging/review environments and reproducible builds;
- no dependency on a single portfolio-only transition system;
- explicit support and incident windows.

### Likely revenue model

- Fixed fee for stable build scopes;
- paid R&D/feasibility sprint;
- day blocks for genuinely embedded variable work;
- reserved-capacity retainer with a strict ceiling;
- specialist subcontract management.

**Planning hypothesis:** USD 3,000–18,000 per bounded build; USD 1,200–3,000 R&D sprint; retainers based on reserved days rather than “unlimited” requests.

### Repeat-client potential

High, provided delivery is dependable and availability can be planned.

### Partnership opportunities

The whole option is partnership-led: brand studios, agencies, producers, motion/3D/sound specialists, CMS/back-end engineers, accessibility reviewers and hosting partners.

### Path from invitations

An invitation alone is weak evidence for this option. The path would need to be:

authored occasion build → credited planner/identity-studio collaboration → public event/hospitality build → agency partner work → repeat campaign capacity.

### Multiverse fit

**Fit: Weak as a lead sales concept; useful as an internal proof of range.**

Agency partners primarily need to assess judgment, craft, stack fit, role, reliability and availability. A cryptic nexus can slow that assessment and imply House Adel prioritizes its own spectacle over the partner’s art direction.

The multiverse could remain a restrained portfolio organization showing unlike realities connected by one production discipline. The shattered-glass nexus is least defensible under this option unless it proves an exceptional, accessible creative-development specimen and leaves conventional navigation visible.

### Evidence needed before selection

- Five partner interviews;
- two paid prototype/build engagements;
- validation of role language and day/fixed-fee preference;
- portfolio/credit term patterns;
- proof that Codex-assisted delivery can meet professional review, testing and maintenance expectations without misrepresenting human capacity.

## Option 5 — The Focused Digital Experience Studio

### Exact description

**House Adel is an independent creative web studio for launches, releases, exhibitions, and occasions—compact digital experiences where art direction, interaction, and story matter more than product-scale infrastructure.**

This is the broadest option and closest to the current project statement. Its promise is a project type and production philosophy rather than one vertical.

### Target clients

- Selected clients from private events, culture, hospitality, products and campaigns;
- planners, curators, artist teams, hospitality founders and independent brands;
- brand/PR/event agencies seeking a compact experience partner.

### Flagship deliverables

- Bespoke invitation and event worlds;
- artist-release and exhibition editions;
- hospitality and product launch chapters;
- compact campaign microsites;
- creative-development prototypes and partner builds.

### Competitive advantage to prove

- One authored House Adel discipline across radically different client realities;
- unusual strength at the junction of art direction and implementation;
- ability to identify when an experience should remain simple;
- asset-first process and partner-shaped production;
- focus on one consequential moment rather than a permanent digital product.

### Advantages

- Preserves the full range of Marshall’s interests.
- Avoids locking the studio into weddings or one cultural niche.
- Gives invitation work a place without making it the whole business.
- Matches the reusable technical core and changing asset-world premise.
- Allows learning from early projects before vertical specialization.

### Weakness

- “Focused digital experiences” is not self-explanatory and can sound like generic agency language.
- Five client types create a difficult sales and referral message.
- Buyers may not see relevant proof quickly.
- The portfolio must be unusually coherent to prevent the offer from looking like unrelated experiments.
- Service design, pricing and partners vary materially by sector.

### Market risks

- Becoming the broad “creative studio” the project explicitly wants to avoid.
- A spectacular multiverse masking a weak or thin body of work.
- Inquiries drifting toward ecommerce, corporate sites, SaaS and general web design.
- No category owning House Adel in referrals.
- Too many speculative portfolio worlds and too little commissioned evidence.

### Portfolio implications

The portfolio would need a highly legible matrix:

- moment: launch, release, exhibition, occasion;
- role: direction, design, development, partner build;
- deliverable and outcome;
- industry secondary, not primary;
- 4–6 deep cases with genuinely different worlds;
- a stable studio/process/about/contact layer;
- explicit non-fit language for large software, ecommerce and generic corporate work;
- a fast conventional work index beside any conceptual nexus.

### Technical implications

- The strongest reusable content/route and asset-manifest system of all options;
- lazy-loaded project-specific code and assets;
- reliable cleanup between technically distinct worlds;
- stable semantic DOM, browser history, deep links and fallbacks;
- flexible CMS/content schema without forcing identical layouts;
- quality tiers and specialist boundaries;
- the portfolio itself must prove versatility without becoming production architecture for client work.

### Likely revenue model

- Paid direction sprint;
- fixed fee by one of the service models;
- partner build fees;
- edition/launch retainers with repeat clients;
- care plan where a live experience has an ongoing role.

**Planning hypothesis:** keep separate quote corridors by buying situation; do not publish one “microsite starting at” price until proposal evidence exists.

### Repeat-client potential

Medium overall; high when the client is a planner, venue, label, gallery, hospitality group, agency or product portfolio rather than an individual.

### Partnership opportunities

Broadest network: planners, event producers, curators, labels, brand/PR studios, photographers/directors, asset specialists, operational platforms and engineers.

### Path from invitations

This option makes the progression explicit:

occasion → event → hospitality/culture → agency-supported commercial launch.

The risk is presenting the end state before the evidence exists. Early public language may need to lead with the one or two categories for which House Adel has real cases.

### Multiverse fit

**Fit: Strong as a portfolio architecture hypothesis.**

This option gives the multiverse the clearest strategic job: show one studio constructing unlike realities while a shared operating system connects them. It could remain relevant as new categories appear.

It also carries the project’s named risks most directly:

- five skins or a theme picker;
- game/Marvel/portal-template associations;
- an effects reel;
- confusing clients who need to identify relevant work;
- expensive asset production for speculative worlds;
- accessibility and mobile compromise.

The multiverse only supports this option if every “universe” is attached to a real project, edition, or disciplined specimen; shared rules are visible; conventional navigation exists; and the metaphor survives beyond the home screen. Shattered glass is one interaction hypothesis, not entailed by the position.

### Evidence needed before selection

- Test the exact one-sentence description with all five buyer groups;
- verify which examples people remember and refer;
- compare inquiry quality against narrower landing pages/decks;
- produce no more than the minimum number of specimens needed to test range;
- evaluate the nexus prototypes independently from brand-language preference.

| **Digital atelier** | Bespoke craft and limited commissions | Strong premium/private tone | Ambiguous, fashion-coded, can sound affected | 1, possibly 3 |
| **Creative-development studio** | Design-aware implementation and technical partnership | Clear to agencies and design teams | Can subordinate art direction and overstate engineering depth | 4 |
| **Digital experience studio** | Broad interactive capability | Familiar to brand buyers | Generic and associated with much larger agencies | 3, 5 |
| **Studio for cultural editions** | Curated, bounded releases | Distinct and conceptually rich | Narrower buyer pool; “edition” may need explanation | 2 |
| **Launch-world studio** | Memorable, project-specific launches | Strong link to the working concept | Risks metaverse/gaming language and spectacle | 3 |
| **Occasion studio / occasion atelier** | Events and intimate milestones | Clear buyer moment | May trap the studio in weddings/private work | 1 |

**Inference:** “House Adel” already supplies some of the warmth, authorship and atelier character. The descriptor may work better when it is plain and specific rather than equally metaphorical.

## Multiverse decision frame across all options

The business position and the portfolio interaction should be approved separately.

Ask:

1. Does the concept make the chosen buyer understand House Adel faster?
2. Does it express a durable truth about the work, not simply current visual taste?
3. Can every new project join without being forced into a portal skin?
4. Does the nexus connect realities while projects retain their own art direction?
5. Is essential work, About and Contact reachable through semantic links immediately?
6. Does it work on mobile, reduced motion, no WebGL and direct project routes?
7. Does it remain compelling after the first 30 seconds, when the visitor needs evidence?
8. Is shattered glass semantically appropriate to the chosen position?
9. Can the studio afford the asset world required to make the metaphor original?
10. Would a simpler threshold/index/constellation/house/archive model express the same truth with less cliché?

No positioning option makes shattered glass inevitable.

## Recommended validation sequence without choosing a winner

1. Create one-page offer sheets for the top three options the creative director wants to test. Keep work samples identical where possible so language is the variable.
2. Conduct structured interviews with at least two buyers and one channel partner per option.
3. Send real, scoped proposals using the quote-test corridors in [`service-models.md`](service-models.md).
4. Record qualified interest, objections, required proof, budget structure, buying owner, timeline and loss reason.
5. Compare three portfolio shells—a conventional work index, a restrained conceptual nexus, and the shattered-fragment prototype—against the same positioning copy.
6. Approve the business position first, then the governing portfolio concept, then the nexus interaction.

## Creative-director decisions still required

- Which buyer should House Adel become easiest to refer first?
- Is the initial portfolio evidence strongest in occasions, culture, launches, or partner development?
- How much white-label work is acceptable?
- Is “House Adel” paired with a plain category descriptor or an atelier/edition/world term?
- Which quote corridor is credible after interviews?
- Which capabilities require named partners before they appear in sales language?
- Does the multiverse clarify the selected option?
- Does shattered glass earn its place against the alternatives?

Until those decisions are made, all five options remain open and the current multiverse/nexus remains a testable hypothesis.

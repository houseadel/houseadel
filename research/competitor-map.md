# House Adel competitor and substitute map

**Status:** Phase 1 market evidence; no competitive position selected  
**Research date:** 2026-07-30  
**Source access date:** All web sources were accessed 2026-07-30.  
**Scope:** This is a commercial map, not a duplicate of the interactive-portfolio landscape. It asks what a buyer can hire or use instead, which organizations are credible partners, and which studios are only future-scale references.

## Evidence labels and classifications

- **Evidence** is linked to an official or authoritative source beside the claim.
- **Inference** is an interpretation, not a sourced fact.
- **Price not public** means no fee or rate card was found on the cited official page during this review; it does not mean the company never shares one privately.

Reference class:

- **Realistic peer:** a small or independent practice whose client and production model House Adel could plausibly meet in an initial phase.
- **Adjacent competitor:** sells to a related buyer but with a different production model.
- **Substitute:** lets the buyer solve enough of the job without commissioning House Adel.
- **Potential partner/channel:** supplies infrastructure, clients, assets, or a route to market.
- **Future-scale reference:** demonstrates a market ceiling but has materially greater team, reputation, client access, or production resources.
- **Narrow reference:** useful evidence for one service or mechanic, not a business model to imitate.

## The competitive field is not one line

Two dimensions matter more than “cheap versus expensive”:

| | Low production breadth | High production breadth |
|---|---|---|
| **Low authored specificity** | DIY template, generic freelancer, smart link | Vertical platform with CRM, RSVP, booking, inventory, or ecommerce |
| **High authored specificity** | **House Adel’s plausible initial territory:** senior-led, compact, collaborator-supported experience | Integrated identity/experience agency or global creative-technology production studio |

**Inference:** House Adel cannot out-feature the vertical platforms and should not pretend to match a 20–40-person studio’s throughput. The viable gap is high authorship with deliberately bounded production.

## Cluster analysis

### A. Free and low-cost template platforms

**Members:** Joy, Minted, Wedera, much of the Indonesian invitation market.

**Surviving value after the first 30 seconds:** logistics. Guests can find the venue, reply, view a schedule, and follow a registry or gift link. The product is successful precisely because it is easy and familiar.

**What House Adel cannot legitimately claim as differentiation:**

- Mobile-friendly;
- customizable color and font;
- RSVP;
- custom URL;
- photo gallery;
- countdown;
- background music;
- map;
- matching invitation;
- “premium template.”

**Market pressure:** Their economics come from software reuse, transactions, cross-selling, or very low production time. A bespoke studio cannot win a feature/price comparison.

**Opportunity left open:** a host with a strong story, professional event team, and desire for a one-off authored world may find the template ceiling visible. House Adel must show that difference immediately and explain the additional production.

### B. Design-forward invitation platforms

**Members:** Bliss & Bone and Riley & Grey.

**Their competitive advantage:** better curation, coherent stationery, privacy/service assurances, and less visual compromise than free all-in-one planning products. Their public prices remain accessible because the underlying system is reused.

**House Adel risk:** describing itself as “editorial,” “luxury,” “customizable,” or “design-forward” puts it directly in this comparison without proving bespoke value.

**Potential distinction:** client-specific concept; original asset direction; no public template catalogue; identity across digital, print, motion, and venue surfaces; direct creative direction; a limited number of commissions.

**Inference:** that distinction will only be credible if case studies expose the brief, decisions, asset provenance, collaboration, mobile/accessibility behavior, and final suite—not only a screen recording.

### C. Indonesian invitation and event-operations vendors

**Members:** Wedera, Wedly, Connectied, and a wider visible market of low-cost template vendors.

**Strengths:**

- Local payment and communication norms;
- WhatsApp-first distribution;
- culturally familiar template choices;
- guest personalization;
- fast fulfillment;
- RSVP, QR check-in, and on-site operations;
- transparent prices.

**Weakness House Adel could exploit carefully:** the offer is normally organized as features, design tiers, and add-ons. There is room above it for an authored occasion system, but only among buyers who already value bespoke production.

**Do not do:** disparage template vendors or hide operational weakness behind aesthetics. Connectied may be more useful as an RSVP/check-in integration or delivery partner than as an enemy.

### D. Vertical operational platforms

**Members:** Paperless Post, Spotify Countdown Pages, Linkfire, Music Glue, Artlogic, Tock, and restaurant site builders.

They solve hard recurring infrastructure:

- delivery and RSVP;
- guest data and check-in;
- streaming destination routing;
- pre-save and release analytics;
- artist commerce and fan data;
- artwork inventory/CRM;
- bookings, payments, and restaurant CRM.

**Strategic conclusion:** use them behind, beside, or after the authored experience. Rebuilding their functions would add risk without improving House Adel’s positioning.

**Integration implication:** every House Adel proposal should identify the system of record, data controller, embedded/linked experience, consent owner, analytics owner, and post-launch operator.

### E. Boutique microsite and creative-development services

RIOT’s public service description—one idea, campaign, launch, or moment in a smaller but concentrated container—is the closest explicit category language found. [RIOT](https://riot.nyc/services/digital/microsites/)

Rhumb Studio is the strongest documented realistic peer in the review. It calls itself a “small but mighty” creative web studio, offers strategy, UX/UI and development, serves premium consumer brands and agencies, uses gated phases, and publishes fixed-fee entry points near €2,000 for landing-page sprints and €12,000 for full-scope work. [Rhumb Studio](https://www.rhumb.co/) It is not a direct invitation or cultural-edition specialist, but it proves that a small international studio can sell clarity, design sensitivity, technical delivery and a fixed process above the template tier.

This cluster remains fragmented: independent developers, brand/web studios, low-code builders, and production partners can all sell “microsites.” One transparent peer does not establish a global median.

**Inference:** House Adel’s competitive advantage here cannot be simply “small,” “independent,” or “creative.” It has to combine:

1. Marshall’s art direction and narrative taste;
2. a bounded asset-first production method;
3. reliable implementation and launch behavior;
4. clear limits and specialist partnerships;
5. case studies demonstrating distinct worlds without losing usability.

### F. Integrated identity and digital studios

**Members:** Studio Dumbar, PORTO ROCHA, Linked by Air, Locomotive, and Studio Freight.

These studios do more than decorate a web deliverable. Their relevant cases show:

- a conceptual rule that generates many assets;
- strategy and content involvement;
- systems that work across formats or editions;
- client/stakeholder collaboration;
- enough production depth to carry the idea through.

They are mostly **future-scale references**, not direct first-year competitors. A prospect comparing House Adel to them will expect stronger proof, project management, and specialist depth than a solo portfolio can imply.

### G. Global creative-technology production

**Members:** OFF+BRAND, Stink Studios, UNIT9.

Their work can include 3D, games, interactive film, regional builds, advertising, product/platform engineering, localization, and large specialist teams. OFF+BRAND’s own team page lists designers, strategists, project managers, CMS specialists, motion designers, a 3D artist, and a WebGL engineer among 39 core team members. [OFF+BRAND about](https://www.itsoffbrand.com/about-us)

**What House Adel can learn:**

- sell a complete campaign role rather than an effect;
- credit collaborators;
- scope localization and regional platforms;
- connect interaction to product or audience behavior;
- show delivery, not only visuals.

**What House Adel should not copy:**

- breadth claims unsupported by permanent staff or contracted partners;
- default WebGL/3D as a status signal;
- global-brand case-study tone before comparable evidence exists;
- production models whose coordination cost exceeds the project.

## Buyer-by-buyer competitive test

| Buyer says… | Most likely substitute | House Adel only wins if… |
|---|---|---|
| “We need guests to RSVP.” | Joy, Paperless Post, Connectied | The project is actually an authored occasion identity and the operations layer is integrated |
| “We want it to look premium.” | Bliss & Bone, Riley & Grey, premium template | “Premium” is made specific through concept, assets, service and suite-wide cohesion |
| “We need a pre-save page.” | Spotify, Linkfire | The real brief includes story, participation, press, fan culture, or a broader release world |
| “We need an online exhibition.” | Artlogic or standard CMS | The curatorial model needs a distinct digital form or reusable exhibition system |
| “We need menu and reservations.” | Squarespace, Wix, BentoBox, Tock | The opening needs place-led art direction, launch content and an authored first impression |
| “We want an immersive fragrance launch.” | Large campaign/experience agency | Scope is compact, assets are excellent, distribution is defined, and specialists are funded |
| “Our main site cannot hold this campaign.” | Landing-page builder or current agency | The separate experience has a strong premise and measurable reason to exist |
| “Our agency designed it; we need someone to build it.” | Creative developer/freelancer | House Adel can protect the design, add interaction judgment, and deliver reliably under partner terms |

## Realistic clients versus aspirational logos

### Realistic near-term

- Destination wedding planner or luxury venue needing a signature digital offer.
- Affluent private client referred by a trusted event professional.
- Independent festival, conference, or awards program with an annual cycle.
- Gallery, curator, foundation, or university cultural program with a bounded exhibition.
- Artist management or independent label with campaign assets and several releases.
- Chef-led hospitality opening already investing in identity, interiors, photography, and PR.
- Independent fragrance/beauty/fashion/object brand with a hero launch.
- Brand, PR, event, or design agency needing a white-label creative-development partner.

### Later, after evidence

- Regional hospitality group with multiple properties.
- Established label roster or major regional artist.
- Museum with procurement and archival requirements.
- Multimarket luxury launch.
- Global entertainment or consumer brand campaign.

### Not an initial sales plan

Chanel, Netflix, Spotify, Coca-Cola, or a national museum direct. Their cited cases are evidence of the *kind of value* buyers commission and the production demands involved, not a lead list.

## Competitive advantage House Adel would need to prove

This is a testable requirement list, not a claim that the advantage already exists:

1. A brief can become a specific governing idea rather than a selected style.
2. Different client worlds can feel radically different while process, accessibility, and delivery remain consistent.
3. AI and licensed assets are directed, documented, and curated rather than exposed as generic generation.
4. A compact team can show exactly which specialist joins and why.
5. Mobile, reduced-motion, no-WebGL, slow-network, and failed-asset states are designed.
6. Operational destinations remain legible and fast.
7. The studio understands the campaign or event calendar, not only launch day.
8. Case studies state role, credits, constraints, and outcomes honestly.

## Unresolved evidence gaps

- No defensible primary-source median fee was found for bespoke invitation art direction, independent campaign microsites, or custom exhibition experiences.
- Public case studies often omit timeline, budget, conversion, accessibility, and maintenance terms.
- The local premium-private-event demand above Connectied’s published custom tiers needs direct validation with planners and venues.
- The willingness of Indonesian artist/label teams and independent hospitality founders to commission standalone experiences needs interviews, not aesthetic inference.
- House Adel has not yet tested which category name produces qualified inquiries.

These gaps should be addressed through 8–12 structured buyer/partner interviews and 3–5 real proposal tests before fixing price, promise, or permanent category language.
